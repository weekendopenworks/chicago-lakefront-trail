About & credits

An independent illustrated guide to the places that make Chicago’s shoreline extraordinary. Here is where the map, the artwork and the photographs come from.

Source code

The website’s source code is available under the MIT License. Geographic data, photographs, illustrations, and fonts are not covered by that license; their separate licenses and permissions still apply.

Geography

The shoreline, mapped walking and bicycle paths, park shapes, roads, beaches, and building footprints use OpenStreetMap data retrieved November 18, 2026. The Park District’s eleven trail-section maps informed the route’s context. Landmarks are placed in a shared latitude-and-longitude coordinate system.

The atlas uses an expanded east–west scale so city blocks, park grounds and shoreline features have room to read. Small illustrated landmarks are anchored to their mapped locations. It simplifies geometry and omits some features. Artwork is a visual interpretation, not a survey, a record of current conditions, or turn-by-turn navigation. Use official trail maps and updates ↗ for travel planning.

Aerial reference

Illustrated geography was reviewed against Cook County GIS aerial imagery and USGS/USDA NAIP imagery, accessed November 18, 2026. These references distinguish built city blocks, lawns, sports grounds, beaches and woodland. The Cook service has inconsistent acquisition-year metadata; the older NAIP survey is from 2019. Neither is presented as live imagery. No aerial basemap is reproduced here.

Tree symbols follow mapped tree locations, and dense canopy is limited to mapped woodland and scrub. A park boundary does not imply forest.

Attraction locations

All 185 attraction pins were reviewed against named map features and official venue, park or institution sources. Statue pins use the mapped statue point; buildings use a point inside the footprint. Beach, harbor and park pins identify an area and do not imply an entrance. Each map card links to its coordinate source. Nearby inland stops require a detour using designated crossings.

Map artwork

Architectural illustrations sit over geographically sourced map layers and may simplify architectural detail. Artwork files, geographic data, and map build scripts are retained with the project.

The revised South Shore Cultural Center vignette is adapted from Zol87’s photograph under CC BY-SA 4.0; the revised 63rd Street Beach House vignette is adapted from TonyTheTiger’s photograph under CC BY-SA 3.0.
